Describe It · Setup ← Home

Start Describe It

Pairs. One phone shows a term and the words you may not use; your partner has to say the term. Students join with the code; the app deals the pairs.

Every term is written with four forbidden words. Show two for a warm-up, four to make them work for it — and you can turn it up between rounds.

The forbidden words are also on the guesser’s screen, with a button to call a foul — otherwise the describer is policing themselves. In a group of three the third student referees instead and the guesser is told nothing, which is the better game: seeing the forbidden words does narrow what the guesser is looking for.

Twenty Questions · Setup ← Home

Start Twenty Questions

Pairs. One phone holds a secret; the partner has twenty yes/no questions to find it. Students join with the code; the app deals the pairs.

Twenty is the game and the right place to start. Come down to fifteen or ten once a class has played it twice — a short limit forces the wide questions first, which is the thinking this activity is for. One subject takes two or three minutes of real questioning. Five minutes is two or three subjects for a working pair.

Only the holder’s phone shows the counter — a student can read their own record and nobody else’s. So the asker has to ask how many they have left, in English, and the holder has to answer. That is the point, not a limitation: both of them end up talking instead of reading a screen.

    Word Partners · Setup ← Home

    Start Word Partners

    Pairs. One phone shows a word and the hidden list of words that go with it; the partner has to say them. Collocation, produced cold rather than picked from four options.

    A card takes about a minute to work through properly. Two minutes is one or two cards for a first round; three is where a confident class ends up.

    The holder has a “That works too” button. A pack lists six partners for a deadline; English has more, and a pair told that “blow a deadline” is wrong has been taught something false by a checklist. Anything that works scores.

    Sets

    The term, then four words the describer may not say — most obvious first. The teacher picks how many of the four a class is held to, so turning the difficulty down removes the last ones rather than the ones that make the item work.
